My boss pulled me into his office and told me he loved me.

That wasn't the concerning part.

"You lead with your heart," he said. "You walk into a room and people notice. But you keep fucking up. I don't know, maybe you need to start meditating or something. I really need you to get it together."

He wasn't wrong.

I started trying to sit still.

For longer than I care to admit, two minutes was the goal. I'm still not particularly good at it. Most days it's less formal meditation and more a minute of gratitude before the day takes over. Seems to be enough.

What changed was the vibe.

Not because I said anything different. I don't think I said anything different. The information just moved differently. People surfaced things earlier. The meeting that used to take an hour took twenty minutes, and the twenty minutes were better.

I want to be careful here, because this is the part where it would be easy to sound like I cracked a code. I didn't. I started paying attention to a variable I'd spent fifteen years treating as a constant.

It gets stranger.

Some leaders walk in and the room opens up. The same people, in the same chairs, will say things they wouldn't say for anyone else. Other leaders walk in and the same room contracts. Not because of what gets said. Something transmits before the words start.

For a long time I thought that was charisma. Some quality you either had or didn't. HeartMath Institute measures it differently. The heart generates the largest electromagnetic field in the body, detectable up to several feet outside of it. Your state changes that field. Other people's nervous systems register the difference before a word gets spoken.

My boss had no idea about any of this. Still knew.

I don't think it's charisma anymore. I think it's state.

The quality of your attention. Whether you're genuinely open to being wrong, or just collecting evidence for what you already think. Whether the people in the room can feel you listening or just waiting. These things are not subtle. They're the loudest signal in the building. Most of us never learn to hear our own.

The question I kept coming back to, and still come back to: if the field a team operates in is partly determined by the state the leader brings into it, then every leadership conversation we're not having is the one about what you're carrying. Pipeline, sure. Activity, fine. But what state did you walk in with today?

I'm still working through it. That's what Field Notes is.

I built something for this. A self-check-in I keep in Google Sheets, the kind I run before a conference, before I'm on stage, before a meeting that matters. Five minutes. No score. Inelegant. Works.

I'm sharing it with you.
